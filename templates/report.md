## Style requirements

Use a neutral professional tone suitable for business review, technical due diligence, or publication.

Required style:

- Write in third person or objective institutional voice.
- Do not address the reader directly.
- Do not cite internal requesters, chat participants, or conversation history.
- Do not include apology language, process commentary, or conversational phrasing.
- Avoid promotional wording such as "best," "game-changing," "clearly," "obviously," or "strongest" unless the basis is quantified and stated.
- Prefer "recommended," "qualified," "primary," "secondary," "higher-evidence," and "lower-intervention" when ranking options.
- Separate evidence from interpretation.
- Label maintainer claims, independent replications, examples, and hypotheses distinctly.
- State limitations and uncertainty in a dedicated section.
- Use straight quotes and plain punctuation.
- Use sentence-case headings.
- Keep boldface for field labels only; do not use boldface for persuasion.

Prohibited content:

- Names of internal stakeholders unless they are formal report authors or public sources.
- Chat-derived references to requester intent, prior corrections, implementation process, or local tool availability.
- Informal reactions, jokes, rhetorical questions, or defensive explanations.
- Uncited claims of superiority.
- Loose bundles of tools framed as stacks without an explicit compatibility rationale.

## Methodology

Describe the evidence sources and evaluation method in publication-ready language. Summarize evidence classes; do not dump raw provenance ledgers or long artifact path lists into the report body.

Recommended evidence-basis categories:

- Repository catalog and backlog:

Raw JSON ledgers, command logs, source-tree dumps, and code-inspection artifacts may remain in the repository for auditability, but professional reports should cite their summarized conclusions through dossier paths, methodology categories, or compact appendix references only when necessary.

README-only inspection is discovery evidence only. Reports that make compatibility or recommendation claims should cite dossier evidence stages and should state when source-logic, benchmark-audit, or reproduction work is still incomplete.
