# Research roadmap

## Current state

The current execution contract is Lifecycle V2 across Fastify and Beets: 6 bounded defect repairs per lane, each restoring one named behavior a specific upstream test decides, under authentic semantic regressions, normal software-engineering prompts, and lenient controller-only acceptance. Every task compiles and also runs one narrow essential-behavior smoke. Fastify requalified on 2026-08-21 against the repaired prompts; Beets was repinned to 746cecf2 and requalified on 2026-08-22.

The previous provider-backed Lifecycle V1 corpus was archived before rerun because model-facing prompts and shared prompt/configuration identity changed. The archive contains 103 historical sessions, their comparisons, and superseded protocols. They remain immutable evidence for the prior bytes and are not current controls.

Both lanes now carry provider-backed results under the current contract. Four interventions were tried against the Beets lane's earlier 22-31% spread -- shared code region, matched task count, prompt precision, and a repin onto a seed-size-bounded task set -- and the first three did not narrow it; the repinned lane has since been measured directly. Further provider work must be fresh and bound to the current Lifecycle V2 qualification hashes. The forward model policy is GPT-5.6 Sol/medium for Codex CLI and OpenCode, and direct-Anthropic Claude Opus 5/medium for Claude Code. High effort is retired for new work because excess deliberation can increase trajectory divergence.

Registry counts, role splits, and runtime splits live in the generated corpus summary in the top-level [`README.md`](../../README.md) rather than being restated here. What that summary cannot say is what the numbers currently support:

- **The three runtimes are not interchangeable controls.** On Fastify each holds baselines whose weighted token cost differs by more than any treatment effect measured so far, so a treatment is only ever read against a baseline from its own runtime. The cross-runtime numbers describe the runtimes, not the tools.
- **Every treatment here is a screen, not an effect estimate.** Caveman, ponytail, and RTK each hold one replicate per lane on each of Claude Code and Codex. Under [ADR 0009](../architecture/decision-records/0009-replicate-counts-are-chosen-not-registered.md) a single replicate can support "worth carrying forward" and cannot support a ranked effect size. Nothing here is yet a ranking, and no two tools may be ordered against each other at these counts.
- **Caveman and ponytail changed weighted cost in opposite directions on the two runtimes, and that is still the strongest pattern in the corpus.** On Claude Code both tools came in below the whole retained baseline range on both lanes: caveman Fastify 543,826 and Beets 342,202, ponytail Fastify 547,110 and Beets 340,309, against baselines of 609,877/657,436 and 478,129/444,683. On Codex neither did: caveman Fastify 830,055 and Beets 711,158 both above the whole baseline range, ponytail Beets 634,381 above it, ponytail Fastify 740,600 inside the 727,939-822,709 spread and therefore indistinguishable from baseline at these counts ([ADR 0007](../architecture/decision-records/0007-ranked-reporting-and-median-sampling.md)). Two unrelated products reproducing the same sign flip points at the runtime rather than at either product. No result from either tool may be reported as its effect without naming the runtime it came from.
- **RTK breaks that pattern, and it is the first tool here to come in below the whole retained baseline range on Codex.** Codex Fastify 687,513 sits below the entire 727,939-822,709 baseline spread and Codex Beets 514,682 below the entire 564,733-614,214 spread, -9.2% and -16.2% against their paired r0s. Its Claude Code half is the weaker one: Fastify 647,992 lands inside the 609,877-657,436 baseline spread and is therefore indistinguishable from baseline at these counts ([ADR 0007](../architecture/decision-records/0007-ranked-reporting-and-median-sampling.md)), while Beets 394,584 is below both retained baselines (478,129/444,683) at -17.5%. So the caveman/ponytail sign flip is a property of those two tools, not a law of the runtimes: a third product ran the other way round.
- **The two RTK halves are different integrations, not the same treatment on two runtimes.** Codex runs `terminal-rtk-codex-instructions-v1`, a product-authored AGENTS.md/RTK.md instruction layer; Claude Code runs `terminal-rtk-claude-code-hook-v1`, the native PreToolUse hook. Both are guide-faithful canonical installs of the same product, but the surfaces differ more than caveman's or ponytail's do across runtimes, so an RTK Codex-versus-Claude-Code gap confounds runtime with integration surface and cannot be attributed to either alone.
- **Explicit adoption tracked the surface almost perfectly.** Under the Codex instruction layer the model wrapped essentially everything it ran — 30 of 31 shell commands on Fastify and 36 of 37 on Beets carried an `rtk` prefix (`rtk git`, `rtk sed`, `rtk rg`, `rtk npm`, `rtk uv`). Under the Claude Code hook it wrapped almost nothing explicitly — 0 of 29 foreground commands on Fastify (one backgrounded shell piped through `rtk grep`) and 7 of 24 on Beets. That is expected rather than anomalous: the hook rewrites below the model-visible command surface, so explicit invocation counts bound natural use under the instruction profile and say little about the hook profile ([ADR 0005](../architecture/decision-records/0005-token-accounting-and-protocol-identity.md)).
- **RTK did not land where caveman and ponytail did, which weakens the shared-lane suspicion below.** On Claude Code Fastify it is 18-19% above both of them (647,992 vs 547,110/543,826) and on Beets 16% above (394,584 vs 340,309/342,202). A third product run through the same Claude Code treatment lane landing well outside their cluster is evidence against the cluster being an artifact of the lane itself — though it does not rule one out, since RTK installs a different surface there than either of them.
- **On Claude Code the two tools landed almost on top of each other, which is a reason for suspicion rather than for confidence.** Caveman and ponytail are within 0.6% of each other on both lanes (547,110 vs 543,826; 340,309 vs 342,202) and within one step on trajectory length (52 vs 52; 35 vs 36). That is far tighter than the Claude Code baselines agree with each other. Two unrelated products converging that closely is more consistent with the Claude Code treatment lane differing from the baseline lane for something common to both runs than with two tools independently producing the same effect. Do not read it as agreement between the tools until a second replicate per profile says whether the gap is stable.
- **Claude Code Beets task-04 yields no verdict on either side of any pair.** All five retained Claude Code Beets sessions — both baselines, caveman, ponytail, and RTK — exit the `beets-membership-query-hashable-v2` verifier with the identical pytest collection error (`OSError: could not get source code` raised from the fixture's own `test/conftest.py` parametrize-id hook), so the verifier never judges the repair. Codex and OpenCode never hit it on the same lane. The pairs therefore still compare equal attempted work, and the earlier reading of this as the treatment doing less work was wrong; what it actually flags is a runtime-dependent fixture defect worth diagnosing before this lane carries more weight.
- **On Claude Code the two cost factors moved against each other for caveman and ponytail, and RTK is the first treatment to move them the other way.** For caveman and ponytail steps fell well below baseline while weighted cost per step rose (caveman Fastify 52 steps at 10,458 and Beets 36 at 9,506; ponytail Fastify 52 at 10,521 and Beets 35 at 9,723; against baselines of 71 at 9,260 and 66 at 6,738), so the totals fell on trajectory length rather than on cheaper steps. RTK Fastify ran 74 steps at 8,757 — slightly more steps than baseline at a lower cost per step, the only Claude Code treatment lane so far where the per-step factor fell — while RTK Beets ran 50 steps at 7,892, cutting trajectory length and paying more per step. On Codex neither factor moved the caveman/ponytail way: caveman Fastify ran 72 steps at 11,528 and ponytail Fastify 68 at 10,891, against baselines of 69-76 steps at 9,972-10,970, so trajectory length stayed in the baseline band and cost per step did the moving. The r0 baselines predate the step instrumentation, so these step figures compare a treatment r0 against a baseline r1.
- **Across RTK's four lanes the factor doing the saving is not consistent, which is the main reason to distrust the mechanism story.** Codex Fastify saved on trajectory length — 64 steps against a 69-76 baseline range, at 10,742 per step inside the 9,972-10,970 baseline band. Codex Beets saved on step price instead — 7,682 per step below the whole 8,824-9,597 band, at 67 steps just above the 63-64 range. Claude Code split the same way but with the lanes swapped. A terminal-output compactor should lower the price of a step; that it does so on only two of four lanes, and lowers step count on the other two, means the totals are not yet explained by the mechanism the product describes. One replicate per lane cannot tell that apart from ordinary trajectory variance.

Two things are worth spending on next, in this order: a second replicate per profile per runtime, because the caveman/ponytail sign flip, their convergence, and RTK's inconsistent cost factor are all one-sample claims that a second sample would either harden or dissolve; and a diagnosis of the Claude Code Beets task-04 collection error, because a lane with a verifier that cannot return a verdict under one runtime is measuring less than it appears to.
